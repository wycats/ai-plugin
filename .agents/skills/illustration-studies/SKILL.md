# Illustration studies

A study should resolve a visual question that matters to the finished page.
Find a complete relationship small enough to examine closely, give the illustrator
room to compose it, and bring the result back into its intended reading context.
Move between the detail and the whole: a local correction can preserve a working
composition while revealing how its neighboring strokes need to change.
The user's judgment of that encounter determines whether we have a useful baseline.

This is experimental contributor guidance for this repository. It is separate
from the published plugin. Use the available image-generation skill for tool
mechanics, saving outputs, and permissions.

## Frame the commission

Begin with the experience the image should support and the relationship that
currently fails. In manuscript lettering, a phrase is drawn for its particular
space. Letter shapes, spacing, line breaks, and neighboring strokes can all
participate in the composition. Naming a historical style supplies a vocabulary;
describing the craft decision explains what that vocabulary must accomplish.

Choose references by what each one demonstrates. Inspect them and state their
roles: edit target, lettering reference, palette, or relationship between writing
and ornament. A successful passage can be a better reference than the latest
whole image. Supply exact words and distinguish the meaning to retain from the
geometry the illustrator may redesign.

For this exploration the user prefers a fresh illustrator agent when a prior
agent has repeatedly missed the brief. Give it the settled commission and selected
references. The coordinator retains comparison and synthesis. Fresh context is
a working preference, not evidence of a different image model or guaranteed reset.

## Make a discriminating study

Pick the smallest complete composition that contains the problem. A title, its
gloss, and their connecting stroke are more informative than a single isolated
letter when the question is how writing inhabits a curve. Give the study enough
image area to inspect, then show it at its intended reading scale too.

When edits stall, compare preserving an existing passage with composing it afresh
from successful style references. Keep wording and the central visual intent
stable. Record other differences: a comparison that changes references and scope
together can guide design without proving which variable caused the result.

Separate structural judgments from color and ornament refinements when those
would obscure the question. Preservation and redesign can conflict: identify what
must stay, and give the interdependent parts room to move together.

## Refine a local relationship

When the whole composition works and one passage does not, isolate the passage
with enough surrounding penwork to explain its place. An enlarged crop can give
the illustrator room to adjust the words and their neighboring strokes together.
The whole image remains the reference for material, scale, and meaning.

Where local compositing is available and authorized, it can establish a better
placement while preserving the rest of the drawing. Let the illustrator then
respond to that placement: a curve may need to rise toward the letters, leave
room for a descender, or continue differently into the next passage. The
composite supplies a concrete relationship to develop, with room for the
interdependent parts to move.

```text
repeat while a specific local question remains:
  compose or draw the passage at a useful scale
  assemble the candidate into the preserved whole when appropriate
  inspect the wording, fit, material, and joining strokes
  if the relationship works in context, return it to the page
  otherwise choose the next study from the visible mismatch
```

An improved crop earns its place through the assembled result. Inspect the
joins and nearby writing as well as the changed phrase; check unchanged regions
directly when exact preservation is part of the commission.

## Read the result together

Inspect the pixels independently of the illustrator's explanation. Show the
reference and result, a close view, and a plausible reading-size view. Assess
wording, letter construction, fit with surrounding penwork, and readability
separately. A change in one does not establish improvement in the others.

Describe visible evidence before giving a verdict. For example, a gloss may use
the desired manuscript hand yet still float above a largely independent curve.
A better local composition may lose the outgoing relationship needed by the map.
Keep both findings available for the user's response.

Repeat only when the next study answers a new question or checks a promising
result. Persistent failure calls for a different representation or production
method, with capabilities checked, rather than an accumulating corrective prompt.

## Return to the page

A promising detail is a candidate, not a finished system. Check whether its
lettering survives reduction and whether it composes with neighboring ideas.
For responsive implementation, distinguish authored artwork from live text and
connections whose geometry follows wrapping, font loading, and resizing.

Preserve the exact prompts, inputs, outputs, and observed limits beside the study.
Keep research claims separate from local observations and possible explanations.
Update this workflow from repeated use and user review. The local return-map
study carried an improved gloss through compositing, a focused drawing pass,
and assembly into the full map; the user accepted it as a working baseline.
That outcome supports this maneuver without establishing its repeatability
across other phrases or compositions. See the [local evidence](local-evidence.md)
when deciding how much confidence to place in the method.

For the research behind this experiment, see
[lettering as a unique composition](https://jessicahische.is/aseriousoversharer),
[path, alignment, and letter deformation](https://www.cs.sfu.ca/~haoz/pubs/zou_sig16_calli.pdf),
and [limits of automated edit assessment](https://research.google/pubs/editinspector-a-benchmark-for-evaluation-of-text-guided-image-edits/).
