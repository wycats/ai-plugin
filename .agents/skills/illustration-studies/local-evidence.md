# Local evidence · 20 October 2026

The return-map study concerned the question “What does this now lead us to
expect?” beneath Hypothesis Forming. The user accepted the wider composition
but found that this gloss drifted away from its supporting line.

A generated single-line question improved the fit while changing the surrounding
sketch material. With the user's authorization, a local composite placed that
question and its nearby strokes into the accepted image. Review found remnants
of the old lettering at the join; revising the mask removed them.

Two subsequent drawing passes on the full composition did little to improve
the remaining pocket below the words. An enlarged crop gave the next illustrator
the title, question, and their immediate penwork. In that result, both the
lettering and lower contour moved, with clearance for the descending p in
“expect.” Compositing this passage back into the full map preserved every pixel
outside the recorded mask.

The coordinator and a separate reviewer inspected the whole map and the joins.
They found a better fit, legible wording, and no visible seam, while retaining a
minor tight spot above the l in “lead.” The user judged the result “pretty good”
and returned the work to the reading experience. The integrated reading now uses
the resulting map.

This is a successful local sequence. The crop, prompt, illustrator context, and
assembly changed during the work, so it does not isolate which intervention
caused the improvement or establish a reliable success rate. The continuous
paired sweep also remained: this result addresses the particular gloss's fit,
not the full range of manuscript composition.
