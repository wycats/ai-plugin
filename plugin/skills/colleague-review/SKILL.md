# Colleague Review

Bring a colleague to the work and let their encounter change how you understand
it. They may infer a relationship you did not intend, need an example you have
been silently supplying, or recognize a consequence your explanation leaves out.
Follow those questions together, then return the resulting understanding to the
artifact. Another reader will eventually encounter it without this conversation.

[Develop the reading together through attention to the shared material](../joint-reading/SKILL.md "composition:load context=activity").
[Examine what someone can understand where they encounter the work](../reader-orientation/SKILL.md "composition:load context=activity").

## Arrange the encounter

Identify what the colleague should be able to understand or do, and choose
someone whose perspective could help examine that. A newcomer may reveal a
missing starting point. Someone expected to extend the work can try whether
the explanation gives them enough to proceed. For prose,
[Editorial Reader](../../agents/editorial-reader.agent.md "composition:reference")
provides a role concerned with the intended audience's understanding.

Keep an informed colleague involved when their accumulated understanding can
help interpret a revision. Invite a fresh reader when you want to examine an
assumption the participants have come to share. Give that reader the purpose,
relevant sources, and room to form an account before adopting your rationale.
If you delegate to an agent, choose its context accordingly and make the scope
of its edits and other actions clear. [Recon](../recon/SKILL.md "composition:reference")
describes how to organize contributions that may need further investigation.

## Let questions shape the discussion

Begin with a concrete part of the work. A colleague examining a visual guide
might first use the interface it describes. An architecture discussion might
follow a code path. A prose reading begins from the intended audience's
knowledge. Choose the material that can support the judgment: a screenshot
shows appearance, while keyboard behavior needs an interaction.

At a meaningful point, invite a question, prediction, or account in the
colleague's own words. Let the response determine what to examine next. Their
question may expose a missing explanation, challenge your understanding, or
show that the distinction already works. Contribute your reasoning and examine
theirs against the passage, behavior, or source that prompted the exchange.

Continue the conversation using the available messaging or shared-reading
surface. When a participant cannot continue, carry their observations, questions,
and the reasons developed together into the next encounter.

## Notice what your explanation supplied

A conversation can make an artifact understandable while exposing what the
artifact still needs to express. If your narration supplies the reason a
publication mechanism matters, consider where that reason belongs on the page.
If source corrects a design gloss, examine the guide that produced the gloss
as well as the individual sentence.

When a finding changes your account,
[revisit the parts whose meaning depended on it](../interpretive-synthesis/SKILL.md "composition:load").
The correction may change the opening, an example, or the order in which ideas
are introduced. When the choice depends on the user's intent or relationship
with the audience, [bring them the passage and the consequential choice](../collaborative-grounding/SKILL.md "composition:load").

## Try what the work should enable

When a guide or design should help someone handle a new situation, ask the
colleague to attempt an unfamiliar application. Begin with the information an
intended contributor would ordinarily have. Further sources or explanations may
help them proceed, and what those additions supplied belongs in your account of
what the guide enabled. Examine the result together. Their explanation of the
worked example may be convincing while leaving an important distinction untested.

For example, a colleague might understand a visual guide's specimens, then
propose a new note whose words express a different idea from its source.
Investigating that difference can improve both the note and the guide's account
of commissioning. Choose a further case for the question it could answer.
The [supporting examples](examples.md) show how these encounters developed in
code, documentation, and visual design.

## Return to the revision

Bring the changed work back to the colleague whose question helped develop it.
Discussion can change their mind. Examine what the revision now communicates
or permits, including places whose meaning changed with the correction.

When judging what the encounter established,
[keep the observations and their conditions available](../observational-grounding/SKILL.md "composition:load").
A simulated reader's reaction, a source check, a tested interaction, and the
user's acceptance establish different things. Preserve the distinction when it
matters to the next decision.

Finish with an account someone can continue from: the consequential finding,
what changed, and any remaining question. Keep useful prose or design choices
when the encounter supports them. Use the project's review process to decide
implementation or release readiness.

[Walkthrough](../walkthrough/SKILL.md "composition:reference") provides a rhythm
for reading code with the user in chunks. It can support an encounter within
this workflow. [PER](../per-cycle/SKILL.md "composition:reference") organizes
preparation, execution, and review when the resulting change needs that wider
coordination.
