# Hypothesis Evaluating

Comparing what was expected with what actually happened, and judging what that
comparison changes about our understanding of the work.

---

The comparison can concern a source inspection, an experiment, an implementation,
or someone's use of the result. Relate what happened to the expectation and
the purpose that made it worth checking. A divergence can expose a mistaken
assumption or a possibility the original plan missed. Agreement can establish
that an approach works while raising a different question about what to do
with it.

Keep the conditions of the observation in view. A successful test may support
the expected behavior for one case while leaving another unexamined. If the
attempt never reached the behavior under test, its result tells us about that
attempt's limits. Explain what the evidence supports before deciding what to
change.

Give evaluation enough attention to form a judgment about the existing result.
A promising revision should not substitute for understanding what happened.
Carry the judgment into the next attempt, then examine that result in turn.

## The relational structure

A scientist reads experimental results against the hypothesis that motivated
the experiment. A predicted effect appears, making a previously doubtful use
of the technique worth investigating. An unexpected effect may instead expose
a missing condition in the theory or a problem with the experiment. The
comparison changes both the strength of the explanation and which experiment
would be useful next.

A forensic accountant reconciles projected cash flows with the actual ledger.
The projection used assumptions about revenue, costs, and timing. Matching
totals may conceal a delayed payment offset by an unexpected receipt. The
accountant examines what produced the totals before judging which assumptions
the result supports and whether the projection remains useful.

A coach reviewing game film against the game plan they drew up before the match. The plan assumed certain matchups, certain defensive formations, certain opportunities. The film shows what actually happened. The coach isn't looking for mistakes — they're calibrating: where did the plan's model of the opponent hold up, and where did the opponent do something the plan didn't account for? The divergences become the foundation for the next game plan.

## Composition notes

Naturally paired with **hypothesis-forming** — one forms the prediction, the other evaluates it against results. The quality of the evaluation depends on the quality of the hypothesis: vague predictions produce vague evaluations, specific predictions produce specific findings.

Composes with **collaborative grounding** when the evaluation surfaces ambiguities that need the user's situated knowledge. Some divergences between prediction and result can only be judged by someone who knows what was intended.

[Observational grounding](../observational-grounding/SKILL.md "composition:reference")
keeps the comparison attached to its sources and conditions.
[Interpretive synthesis](../interpretive-synthesis/SKILL.md "composition:reference")
relates the result to the larger understanding, including when an accurate
prediction acquires a different significance.
