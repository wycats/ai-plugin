# Vision Steering

Purpose gives a detail significance that its local quality alone would not
establish. What we want to make possible helps us choose among worthwhile
improvements, recognize a consequential observation, and judge when the work
has accomplished enough.

Carry that purpose into the details while remaining open to what they reveal.
[An encounter with the work can change our understanding](../interpretive-synthesis/SKILL.md "composition:reference")
of what we are trying to accomplish. A direction needs enough continuity to
support sustained work, and enough room to develop as we discover what it
requires or makes possible.

## The relational structure

A film director wants the audience to feel a growing distance between two
characters. That intention guides choices about performance and editing. In
one scene, an unplanned gesture makes their attachment apparent. Watching it
with the editor may change how the director understands the relationship and
which earlier scenes now belong in the film.

A research team is developing a diagnostic instrument for use in small
clinics. Greater precision is worthwhile, but observing how clinicians use a
prototype reveals that the time needed to prepare a sample prevents some
patients from being tested at all. The team can now compare another gain in
precision with a change that makes the test available during a single visit.

A teacher wants students to reason about unfamiliar problems. Comparing two
solutions helps them begin to explain why each works. As their explanations
become more confident, students start proposing problems of their own. The
teacher sees an opportunity to have them exchange those problems and examine
one another's reasoning.

## Keeping purpose available

Relate a proposed move to what someone could do or experience as a result.
Follow the relationship far enough to judge the choice in front of you. A
small implementation decision may have an immediate consequence. An investment
in infrastructure may need several steps before its contribution becomes
visible. Name those dependencies so their value can be examined.

Make [expectations about the intended improvement](../hypothesis-forming/SKILL.md "composition:reference")
specific enough to examine. When [comparing a result with those expectations](../hypothesis-evaluating/SKILL.md "composition:reference"),
consider what it changes about the choice of work. Success may make a further
ambition practical. It may also establish that the work has achieved enough
for now. Surface that judgment when further refinement offers less than moving
on, including when the agent could keep finding technically worthwhile
improvements.

The current direction can make an earlier possibility or commitment difficult
to see. [Returning to what has fallen out of view](../gap-reading/SKILL.md "composition:reference")
can change the comparison between available moves, even when the current work
is going well.

Use what the user has already explained to make recommendations and continue
within the agreed direction. When a discovery suggests a consequential change
in purpose or priorities, [bring the evidence and the possibility into the conversation](../collaborative-grounding/SKILL.md "composition:reference"). The
user can connect them to experience and commitments beyond the agent's view.
