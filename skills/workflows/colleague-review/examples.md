# Encounters that changed the work

These examples are condensed from development conversations with agent
colleagues. They illustrate how a question changed what the participants
examined and how that understanding returned to the artifact.

## An architecture explanation meets its implementation

Two readers followed a code path in a documentation engine. An explanation
called publication “promotion as one unit.” One reader asked what happened
between moving the existing output aside and installing its replacement.
Tracing the implementation exposed an attempted rollback after a failure,
which called for a more precise account of the guarantee.

Another reader noticed that a render callback also committed source-cache
entries and retained private payloads. The callback's apparent role did not
explain those effects. The discussion exposed a lifecycle contract that the
architecture account had compressed. Questions about the sampled code
changed the next part of the walkthrough.

## A guided explanation supplies something the page omits

Readers examined screenshots of the same documentation. They understood the
payoff of nested expansion but could not tell whether “Project” meant authored
configuration or a resolved internal model. The coordinator's narration also
supplied a reason for the publication mechanism that the page had not given.

Those exchanges showed where the page needed to carry more of the explanation.
Source could settle a question about the underlying model. The captured screens
could show its presentation. A claim about keyboard interaction still needed
someone to try that interaction.

## A visual guide meets a new commission

A colleague read an illustrated guide and proposed a note for Diagnostic
Questioning. The proposed words described hypothesis testing. Returning to the
canonical stance clarified the difference and changed both the commission and
the guide's instructions for developing one.

A subsequent commission for Observational Grounding kept observations from
different surfaces distinct. That provided evidence about applying the written
guide. The illustration itself still needed to be drawn and examined.

## A colleague continues through revisions

An architecture colleague stayed involved as investigation and implementation
workflows were revised. In one draft, a participant had to formulate a detail's
significance before bringing it to another investigator. The colleague pointed
out that the exchange itself might be how they discover its significance.

Later revisions were read in light of that finding. A new sentence made a
well-formed question the prerequisite for asking for help. Recognizing the
same assumption in new wording required remembering why the earlier passage
had changed, then examining what the new passage actually said.
